# Fact base: maximising sponsorship cash for a major college fest in a Tier-1 Indian city

Compiled 2026-11-26 (public sources only) from six research streams, each with an adversarial fact-check.

**Rules applied**
- Refuted claims are dropped and listed in §7b.
- Where the fact-check corrected a value, the corrected value is used.
- Claims that could not be checked are marked **UNVERIFIED** and also appear in §7.
- Every number has a URL or is labelled **ESTIMATE**.
- "Asking" means a published rate card, not a deal that was actually paid.


---

## 0. Five facts that should drive the case

1. **Sponsorship cash comes from a few deals.** The only line-item actuals found are Antaragni'11 (IIT Kanpur). There, the top 6 of 44 deals gave 54.4% of sponsorship cash, deals of ₹1L or more gave 69.5%, and the median deal was ₹49k ([IITK finance report](https://www.iitk.ac.in/gymkhana/Senate/9th%20Meeting/AntaragniFinanceReport-16.03.pdf), high confidence, 2011).
2. **No fest has published realised (actually paid) deal values after 2015.** Every 2023-26 rupee figure is an asking price. The defensible title-deal ESTIMATE for a flagship IIT fest is **₹50-75L**. Anything above ₹1 crore is speculative.
3. **Banks, fintech and UPI companies dominate 2024-26 title slots.** Examples: NPCI (Techkriti'26), POP UPI (Mood Indigo'25), IDFC FIRST, Union Bank, SBI ([monumentum.in](https://monumentum.in/blog/college-fest-sponsors-india), medium confidence).
4. **Mumbai and Delhi-NCR are tied** (8.90 vs 8.95 after correcting the venue scores). The sponsor mix should decide between them.
5. **Collection risk is material.** Antaragni'11 still had 7.4% of income uncollected when the finance report was filed. Indian corporate DSO is 56-75 days. The plan needs advance or milestone billing.

**Derived benchmarks and estimates**
- **Title deal at a top-10 IIT fest (2025-26), ESTIMATE:** ₹50-75L central range; above ₹1 cr is speculative. Basis: Rendezvous' ~₹20L title (2007, **UNVERIFIED**) multiplied by ~3x CPI inflation (estimate). The ksverse blog figures (title ₹1-3 cr, Mood Indigo budget ₹10-12 cr, Shaastra ₹5-8 cr) are unsourced and should be treated as upper bounds only ([ksverse](https://ksverse.in/blogs/culture/iit-fest-budgets-mood-indigo-shaastra), low confidence).
- **Total sponsorship cash at flagship Mumbai and Delhi IIT fests, ESTIMATE:** ₹3-8 cr each (low confidence). Basis: Techfest's ₹1.6-2 cr in 2014-15, grown forward.
- **Share of value by tier, ESTIMATE** (from Antaragni'11 actuals plus rate cards):
  - title and co-title 25-40%
  - associate, presenting and powered-by 20-30%
  - event and night titles 20-30%
  - stalls and small partners 5-10%
  - in-kind deals are 30-60% of logos but less than 10% of cash
- **Market size, ESTIMATE:** college-fest sponsorship is ~₹65-275 cr a year, under 2% of India's ₹13,000 cr live-events market ([IBEF](https://www.ibef.org/news/india-s-us-1-41-billion-live-events-market-fuelling-experiential-marketing-push-bookmyshow-ey-report)). A ₹17,000 cr figure for that market is **UNVERIFIED**.
- **Unbundling beats a single title.** Ignisense prices its Cultural Night (₹5L) above its fest title (₹4L). Mood Indigo 2025 sold 10 night and competition titles. Techfest 2026 and Saarang 2026 sold vertical or co-title packages instead of one whole-fest title.
- **Footfall claims are unreliable.** The same fest is cited at 1.04L-1.63L in different sources, and Rendezvous at 60k-1.6L. Most claims are self-reported or pre-event projections. Verified reach metrics are a selling point.

## 2. City recommendation

**Recommendation:** Mumbai and Delhi-NCR are tied, and both are clearly ahead of Bengaluru.

- **Primary pick: Mumbai, if the title strategy is led by banks and fintech** (the dominant 2024-26 title buyers, §1/§3). Mumbai is the main FMCG and agency HQ cluster and has India's two most-sponsored fests as proof that brands spend there. Hold the fest outside Mood Indigo and Techfest season (mid to late December; Mood Indigo runs 21-24 Dec 2026), or pitch exclusivity because the fest is off-cycle.
- **Pick Delhi-NCR if the sponsor list is led by D2C, quick-commerce or consumer startups.**
- **The 0.05-point gap between the two is within scoring noise.**

**What this means for the choice**
- Students are plentiful in all three top cities. The binding constraint is how close the fest is to the people who approve brand budgets.
- Venue cost differences (₹30k to ₹1.5L a day) are small next to sponsorship differences measured in tens of lakhs. Optimise for the sponsor pool first and use an on-campus venue to keep cost near zero.
- Brand financial years close on 31 March, so a December-February fest can catch unspent Q4 budgets. This is reasoning, not a sourced fact.

### GST and TDS impact on cash

**GST**
- Sponsorship services carry 18% GST (SAC 998397) ([Bajaj Finserv](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/gst-on-sponsorship)).
- Notification 07/2025-CT(Rate), effective 16 Jan 2025 ([GST Council](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/node/5522)):
  - If the supplier is a **body corporate**, it charges GST itself (forward charge).
  - If the supplier is not a body corporate (society, trust, AOP) and supplies a body corporate, the sponsor pays GST under reverse charge (RCM).
- WB AAR 22/WBAAR/2025-26 (10 Dec 2025) held that a Section 8 company **is** a body corporate and must charge GST on forward charge ([ruling](https://comtax.wb.gov.in/GST/GST_Advance_Ruling/22WBAAR2025-26_20251210.pdf)). It supports the plain reading of the notification; it does not contest it.

**TDS**
- Sponsorship is treated as an advertising contract, so TDS falls under s.194C (CBDT Circular 5/2002) ([Income Tax India](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/5/2002-circular-no.-5/2002-dated-30-07-2002)).
- From 1 Apr 2026 this is s.393(1) Table 6(i): 1% if the payee is an individual or HUF, 2% for others. No TDS if a single payment is ≤₹30,000 and the year's total is ≤₹1,00,000 ([Taxguru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1052630); thresholds single-source).
- 20% if the payee has no PAN (**UNVERIFIED**).
- TDS is computed excluding GST when GST is shown separately (CBDT Circular 23/2017) ([PDF](https://itatonline.org/info/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/CBDT-Circular-23-TDS-GST.pdf)).

**Worked example for a ₹1,00,000 deal (arithmetic)**

| | Fest is a society or trust (RCM) | Fest is a Section 8 company or other body corporate (forward charge) |
|---|---|---|
| Invoice | ₹1,00,000 | ₹1,18,000 |
| Who pays GST | Sponsor pays ₹18k under RCM | Fest collects ₹18k and remits it |
| Cash received | ~₹98,000 after 2% TDS | ₹1,16,000 (TDS ₹2k on the pre-GST value) |
| Net cash | **~₹98,000** | **~₹98,000**, plus GST registration and compliance |

The TDS can be claimed back as a tax credit only if the entity files returns.

**Risks to manage**
- If a sponsor wrongly deducts TDS under s.194J at 10%, the fest loses 8 extra points of cash. Cite Circular 5/2002 in the MoU.
- Put the fest entity's PAN on every invoice.

### Funnel and forecast ESTIMATES (not sourced; derived from the benchmarks above)

- **Per 100 cold prospects** (email plus LinkedIn): 12-20 conversations, 8-12 proposals, **1-3 signed**.
- **Per 100 warm intros** (alumni, faculty, past sponsors): 40-60 meetings, 30-45 proposals, **10-20 signed**. Lapsed or past sponsors re-sign at 30-50%.
- **Slippage:** 10-25% of verbally confirmed value never becomes a signed MoU or payment. Of signed value, 60-80% arrives before the fest unless 100% advance is enforced.
- **Stage weights for forecasting:** cold 10%, proposal 20-40%, verbal 80%, signed 95%, cash received 100%. A 45% "committed deals slip" figure from an aggregator is **UNVERIFIED**. Gartner's 45% is about forecast confidence, not slippage ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2020-02-12-gartner-says-less-than-50--of-sales-leaders-and-selle)).
- **North-star metric:** cash in bank by T-14 days before the fest, not "confirmed" value.
- **Timing (as of 2026-11-26):** a fest in Jan-Mar 2027 is 2-4 months away. That window mostly supports renewals and warm 3-5 week cycles. New logos need 8-14 weeks.
